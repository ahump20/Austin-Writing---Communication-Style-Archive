# Austin Humphrey Writing Record

A living archive and operational spec for **Austin Humphrey’s** writing voice: the pre‑AI corpus (human‑only) plus the modern AI‑assisted era, merged into one usable system.

This repository exists for one reason: **so new writing continues to sound like Austin, even when tools, mediums, and stakes change.**

---

## What this is

- **A voice archive**: representative samples across time, genres, and stakes.
- **A voice specification**: repeatable rules, patterns, and anti‑patterns.
- **A working toolkit**: prompts, rubrics, and auditing procedures that make “write like me” executable.

## What this is not

- **Not** a Blaze Sports Intel brand guide. BSI writing appears here as *Austin’s* applied voice in a sports context, but the goal is Austin’s underlying engine.
- **Not** a stat database or a citation warehouse. (Some samples include sourcing discipline as part of voice, but facts live with their sources.)

---

## How to use this in 10 minutes

1) **Pick the register** you’re writing in:
   - Personal / reflective
   - Analytical / argumentative
   - Strategic / business
   - Sports / narrative‑analytics hybrid

2) **Draft using Austin’s paragraph rhythm**: **Anchor → Expand → Drive**
   - *Anchor* (4–10 words): claim / position
   - *Expand* (20–40+ words): mechanism + evidence + qualification
   - *Drive* (10–18 words): implication / next move

3) **Run the Humphrey Writing Index (HWI)**
   - If it scores below the threshold, fix the lowest two categories first.

4) **Ship with credibility discipline**
   - Concrete facts where they matter.
   - No hype.
   - Confidence calibrated to evidence.

## Austin Voice DNA

These are the stable traits that show up across eras and genres.

1) **Systemic lens**
   - Writes events inside systems (political, economic, cultural) and traces causal flow.

2) **Common man as protagonist**
   - Even in abstract topics, the analysis returns to how systems land on real people.

3) **Declarative confidence**
   - Claim first; evidence in service of the claim. One hedge max, then commit.

4) **Counterintuitive finding**
   - Loves second‑order effects: the mechanism designed to solve X creates Y.

5) **Theory serves practice**
   - Frameworks only appear to *do work* on a concrete case.

6) **Evidence at the point of need**
   - Data lands where the argument needs it, not dumped at the beginning.

7) **Compressed syntax**
   - Semicolons, colons, parentheticals, and parallel tracks used as structure, not decoration.

8) **Conclusive reframe**
   - Endings widen the lens and point forward; they don’t summarize.

9) **Comparative structure**
   - Insight generated through contrast: X vs Y, expected vs actual, surface vs system.

10) **Physical objects + places as emotional anchors**
   - Meaning carried by concrete locations or objects rather than emotional inflation.

## Anti‑patterns (fast drift detectors)

If these show up, it’s usually not Austin.

- Hedge stacking (“might perhaps potentially…”)
- Passive academic sludge (“it has been observed…”)
- Hype language (revolutionary, game‑changing, best‑in‑class)
- Filler openers (“In today’s rapidly evolving landscape…”)
- Summary endings (“In conclusion, this paper has shown…”)
- Bullet‑point prose (lists replacing reasoning)
- Disconnected theory (framework with no immediate application)

## Using AI without losing Austin’s voice

AI is a power tool. This archive is the safety guard.

- Use AI for **compression**, **structure checking**, **alternate phrasing**, **outline stress‑tests**, and **copy edits**.
- Avoid letting AI invent the “engine.” Austin’s voice comes from:
  - claim pressure
  - system logic
  - concrete grounding
  - a real turn
  - a forward close

**Rule of thumb:** if a paragraph has no causal mechanism, no specific anchor, and no implication, it’s not finished.
