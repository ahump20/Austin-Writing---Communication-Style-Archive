The No. 6 Texas Longhorns left San Antonio with a win worth keeping and several possessions worth watching again as the [75-72 victory over the Baylor Bears](https://texaslonghorns.com/news/2026/11/15/no-7-7-mens-basketball-tops-baylor-75-72-in-san-antonio-hoops-showdown) required contributions beyond the leading scorer — Isaiah Johnson supplied 26 points, Matas Vokietaitis finished the possession that put Texas ahead for good, and Elyjah Freeman helped close it at the free-throw line.

Thursday’s game against the Southern Utah Thunderbirds gives head coach Sean Miller’s team, now 4-0, an opportunity to carry those contributions forward while addressing the problems that kept the Bears close.

Tip is set for 7 p.m. Central at the Moody Center, with SEC Network+ carrying the game.

The opponent changes, but the work Miller identified after the Baylor game still needs doing.

#### Southern Utah’s scoring average needs context

The Thunderbirds enter at 3-2 and are averaging 96.8 points per game. That number deserves a second look before anyone uses it to predict Thursday’s pace or difficulty.

Southern Utah scored 134 points against West Coast Baptist and 143 against Bethesda. Neither is a Division I program, and neither is Grace, which the Thunderbirds beat 97-53. Their two games against Division I opponents produced very different results: an 88-59 loss at High Point and an 89-51 loss at Wake Forest.

[Bart Torvik’s ratings](https://barttorvik.com/team.php?team=Southern+Utah&year=2027), which count only games against Division I opponents, had Southern Utah 257th of 365 teams through Wednesday, with an offense ranked 237th and a defense ranked 287th in adjusted efficiency. Fourth-year head coach Rob Jeter rebuilt the roster with six transfers after a 10-22 season, and the program is back in the Big Sky Conference after four years in the WAC.

In those two road losses, Southern Utah shot a combined 34.9 percent from the field and 25 percent from three-point range. The Thunderbirds also committed 25 turnovers at High Point. Texas has an opportunity to disrupt their offense without needing to gamble for steals.

The misses still need collecting. Southern Utah grabbed 17 offensive rebounds against High Point and 13 against Wake Forest. [Southern Utah’s official statistics](https://suutbirds.com/sports/mens-basketball/stats/2026-27) make that a more relevant concern than its inflated scoring average. By Torvik’s accounting, the Thunderbirds have rebounded 36.1 percent of their own misses against Division I opponents, which ranks 100th nationally, while allowing those opponents to rebound 52.9 percent of theirs, the worst mark in Division I through Wednesday.

Anthony Breland gives the Longhorns a shooter to locate when Southern Utah turns to its bench. He leads the Thunderbirds at 12.8 points per game and has made 11-of-25 three-pointers, 44 percent. Jajuan Nicholls averages 11.4 points and leads the team with 5.4 rebounds per game. His 12-point, 10-rebound performance against Wake Forest showed he could still produce in a difficult matchup.

Texas should have opportunities to take control. The more useful question is whether the Longhorns will begin with the aggression they needed time to find against Baylor.

#### Rebounding has to finish the possession

Before the game, Miller said preparing for Baylor starts with its offensive rebounding. While the final rebounding margin suggested Texas had handled that part of the assignment, Miller’s assessment was less comfortable.

“We won it, but we didn’t win it,” Miller said afterward. “They had 17 second shots.”

Texas finished with a 41-39 advantage, but Baylor collected 17 offensive rebounds. The Longhorns had 12.

Where Miller could point to better success is in his team’s second-chance defense as Baylor was only able to turn those 17 offensive rebounds into 12 second-chance points, an area where Texas was more efficient on the other end with 18 second-chance points.

Even so, allowing that many additional opportunities meant the Longhorns repeatedly had to defend beyond the first missed shot — that’s a lot of lost possessions in a close game.

The raw count needs its own context. Baylor missed 42 shots from the field to Texas’ 28, so there were more rebounds available on its end, and by rate the teams were close: The Bears rebounded 37 percent of their own misses, the Longhorns 35 percent of theirs. That is still well above the 28.7 percent Texas has allowed this season, [per Torvik](https://barttorvik.com/team.php?team=Texas&year=2027), a figure that ranks 118th nationally.

Miller connected Baylor’s runs to those unfinished possessions and wants better defensive rebounding from his backcourt. He also credited the opponent, calling rebounding the thing Scott Drew’s teams have historically done best.

For Vokietaitis, Miller described a specific adjustment — keeping the ball high after securing it instead of bringing it down into traffic. That makes it harder for a defender to reach in and gives the outlet pass a cleaner start.

Against Southern Utah, that is a useful standard regardless of the score. Force the miss, secure the rebound, and get into the next possession without giving the opponent another opening.

#### Keep finding more than one way to contribute

Johnson’s shooting carried considerable weight against Baylor. He made all four of his three-point attempts and finished 8-of-14 shooting from the field.

His teammates helped create the conditions for that production.

Austin Goosby recorded seven assists and six rebounds despite foul trouble. Miller pointed to the value of Goosby’s playmaking in allowing Johnson to work as a scorer rather than having to create every opportunity himself — working off the ball produces more catch-and-shoot looks for Johnson, higher percentage than shots taken off the dribble.

The decisive possession illustrated the benefit of having another player make the read. Goosby found Vokietaitis for the dunk that put Texas ahead 73-72 with 33 seconds remaining, a “Pistol-Spain” set with Johnson setting the back screen that got Vokietaitis open.

Freeman’s contributions were just as important to the finish. He scored 10 points, added three rebounds and three assists without a turnover, and made the two free throws with five seconds left that established the final margin.

Miller emphasized Freeman’s second-half involvement after the game — his late rebounding and decisions with the ball mattered alongside his scoring.

The bench also helped Texas move beyond its tentative start. Miller credited Marcus Spears Jr.’s energy as part of that response. Thursday offers a chance to establish that activity at the opening tip rather than wait for substitutions to change the game.

Texas remains without guard Mikey Lewis, who is recovering from surgery on a broken fifth metatarsal. Miller said last week that Lewis reaches the eight-week mark around next week’s trip to San Diego and that a return in the second or third week of December is possible.

#### Don’t let the result hide the remaining work

Baylor made 11-of-25 three-pointers. Texas missed eight free throws, finishing 15 of 23 at the line, six days after going 27 of 42 against Grambling State. The Longhorns rank 33rd nationally in free-throw rate, per Torvik, and 245th in free-throw percentage at 68.3 percent.

The Baylor game gave a new team experience making decisions under pressure. Texas found the go-ahead basket, protected its lead, and made the final free throws. Those are useful possessions to have played together.

One of them was by design. Leading by three in the final seconds, Texas fouled instead of letting Baylor attempt a tying 3-pointer, and the Bears missed both free throws. Miller said the Longhorns have worked on that situation since early in the fall.

“Like all of us as coaches, your hardest lessons are in your worst moments,” Miller said.

His came in the 2007 NCAA Tournament, when his Xavier team [led Ohio State by three in the final seconds](https://ohiostatebuckeyes.com/news/2007/3/17/lewis-pushes-ohio-state-past-xavier), did not foul and gave up Ron Lewis’ tying three-pointer. The Buckeyes won in overtime and reached the national championship game. Miller said he has fouled in that spot ever since.

“I would rather go down the other way than watching that deep three go in at the buzzer,” he said.

Southern Utah presents a different test of what the Longhorns took from San Antonio. The opportunity is to sustain the details that helped them finish while reducing the mistakes that made the finish necessary.

Texas does not need another tight game to prove it learned something against Baylor. An aggressive start, completed defensive possessions, and the same willingness to create for a teammate would make the point just fine.
